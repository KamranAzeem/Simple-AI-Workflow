# Simple AI Workflow v6.6.0

Released 2026-11-18.

## The common policy got smaller

`ai-policy-common.md` had grown to 869 lines. Every session loaded all of it, including rules you only need when you write an issue, clean up knowledge, or draft a design.

I split it. The common policy keeps the rules that apply to every session. The task rules moved to their own policies and load when the task needs them.

## What moved

- Outbound content scrub.
- Knowledge rules.
- Issue management.
- Design documents.
- Grilling and brainstorming.

The state-file rules stay in the common policy. So do the engineering and testing standards, because every session needs them.

The boundary is one way: a plug-in may cite the common policy, and the common policy names no plug-in.

## How a policy loads

A policy can declare when it loads in an optional `## Loading` section, one path per policy: `boot`, `trigger`, `procedure`, or `expertise`. A policy with no declaration keeps its old behavior. Your project policies keep the boot load, so you do not need to change anything. A procedure can still load a policy by name.

The authoring validator and `tests/test-policy-load-paths.sh` check that every declared path is real. A procedure that names a missing policy fails the suite.

## Your own policies

The [custom policy howto](../custom-policy-howto.md) covers the file shape, the four modes, validation, and migration. Put a custom policy in your project root `ai/policies/`, give it the standard shape, and validate it with the clone's validator.
